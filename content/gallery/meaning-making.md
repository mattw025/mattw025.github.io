My research on seventeenth-century Britain examines how people understood time and the future during periods of political crisis. I focus on the execution of Charles I in 1649 and the responses of astrologers, historians and newsbook authors to a sudden rupture in the political order.

By reading almanacs, newsbooks and histories across time, I trace how writers adapted familiar ideas about providence, monarchy, generations and cyclical rebellion to explain the regicide. This work underpins my current book manuscript and has appeared in [*Historical Research*](https://doi.org/10.1093/hisres/htab029) and a [Brill volume on monarchy in early modern Europe](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789004694149_005).

A related strand of research examines how ideas about place shape political possibility. My MSc research analysed the geographical imaginations embedded in the negotiations of the Antarctic and Outer Space Treaties.
