I use GIS, data visualisation and computer vision to recover information that is difficult to see in large collections of historical sources. The methods serve historical questions about how places were named, represented and understood.

As Digital Postdoctoral Research Assistant on the AHRC-funded [*Brightening the Covenant Chain*](https://treatiedspaces.com/diplomacy-and-treaties/) project, I processed British Library maps of colonial North America to create a dataset for an interactive Kinetic Map. Developed with the Treatied Spaces Research Group and King’s Digital Lab, the map traces Indigenous and non-Indigenous movement, settlement and diplomacy across the northeast.

An Alan Turing Postdoctoral Enhancement Award supported my collaboration with [The Alan Turing Institute](https://www.turing.ac.uk) on high-performance computing and computer vision for early modern maps. At NUS, I also contribute to a project that extracts meteorological observations from ships’ logbooks to study historical rainfall patterns in Southeast Asia.
