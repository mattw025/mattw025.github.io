**Historical GIS:** I use historical geographical information systems (GIS), georeferencing, and data capture on historic maps. For the *Brightening the Covenant Chain* project, I processed maps of colonial America from the British Library to create a dataset for a digital interactive [Kinetic Map](https://treatiedspaces.com/digital-storytelling/#kinetic-map). The visualisation traces how treaty-making transformed the political and intercultural landscape of northeastern North America over 230 years.

**Computer vision and historical maps:** I collaborated with the [Alan Turing Institute](https://www.turing.ac.uk) to explore how high-performance computing and computer vision could recognise text in early modern maps and trace Indigenous contributions to European cartographic knowledge. This work was funded by an Alan Turing Post-Doctoral Enhancement Award.

**Crowd-sourced and environmental data:** I am co-lead on [Mapscapes](https://treatiedspaces.com/digital-storytelling/#zooniverse-platform), a people-powered research project that invites volunteers to recover the Indigenous cultural geography of northeastern North America. Using historical sources, volunteers locate the names used by the Haudenosaunee and other Indigenous nations for villages, towns, and significant places within their homelands. I also contribute to a project on rainfall trends in Southeast Asia by digitally extracting and compiling meteorological data from ships’ logbooks.
