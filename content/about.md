I am a cultural historian of early modern Europe and [Senior Tutor](https://discovery.nus.edu.sg/6890-matthias-wong) in the [Department of History](https://fass.nus.edu.sg/hist/) at the National University of Singapore. My research focuses on seventeenth-century Britain, temporality, and reactions to trauma in print culture. I also work in public history, [digital humanities](#digital-humanities), and [environmental research](#environmental-humanities).

My current book manuscript examines the execution of Charles I in 1649 as a moment of ideational change and adaptation. It traces how astrologers, history writers, and newsbook authors understood the future, and how their visions shifted after an unexpected and violent regicide. This research developed from my PhD in History at the [University of Cambridge](https://www.hist.cam.ac.uk). My MSc in International Relations at the [S. Rajaratnam School of International Studies](https://www.rsis.edu.sg) examined the geographical imaginations embedded in the Antarctic and Outer Space Treaty negotiations.

My digital humanities research uses GIS, visualisation, and computer vision to recover Indigenous American presence and cultural geography from historical documents. As a postdoctoral researcher with the [Treatied Spaces Research Group](https://treatiedspaces.com) at the University of Hull, I processed maps of colonial America for the *Brightening the Covenant Chain* project and its Kinetic Map visualisation. I worked with [King’s Digital Lab](https://kdl.kcl.ac.uk) and collaborated with [The Alan Turing Institute](https://www.turing.ac.uk) on the use of high-performance computing and computer vision to trace Indigenous cartographic contributions in early modern maps. I also contribute to a project on historical rainfall trends in Southeast Asia by extracting and compiling meteorological data from ships’ logbooks.

As a public historian, I have worked with the [American Museum & Gardens](https://americanmuseum.org) and the [North American Native Museum](https://www.stadt-zuerich.ch/kultur/en/index/institutionen/native_american_and_inuit_cultures.html) to enhance the interpretation of Indigenous materials and explore digital connections between communities and collections. I co-led a university consortium project on the social, cultural, and environmental impacts of UK outer-space activities. I have also worked with Pearson Edexcel and AQA on history curriculum reform, and co-authored evidence and provided research expertise to the UK Parliament.

At NUS, I teach cultural astronomy, European history, digital history, and public history. In 2025, I helped oversee *Beyond the Page*, a student-led oral-history and exhibition project about the NUS Department of History. I also convened the Singapore iteration of [*Walking Through a Songline*](https://fass.nus.edu.sg/hist/songline/), presented with the National Museum of Australia at NUS Central Library in 2025–26.

I am Associate Editor of [*Elements in Indigenous Environmental Research*](https://www.cambridge.org/eier), published by Cambridge University Press. I am an Associate Fellow of the [Higher Education Academy](https://www.advance-he.ac.uk) and the [Royal Historical Society](https://royalhistsoc.org), and a Member of the [AHRC Peer Review College](https://www.ukri.org/councils/ahrc/guidance-for-reviewers/peer-review-college).
