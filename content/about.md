I am a cultural historian and [Senior Tutor](https://discovery.nus.edu.sg/6890-matthias-wong) in the [Department of History](https://fass.nus.edu.sg/hist/) at the National University of Singapore. My research asks how people make sense of rupture, time, and contested places. Its historical core is seventeenth-century Britain, where political crisis forced writers to rethink the relationship between past, present, and future.

My current book manuscript examines the execution of Charles I in 1649 as a moment of intellectual change and adaptation. It traces how astrologers, historians, and newsbook authors interpreted an unprecedented regicide, and how they placed it within longer narratives of providence, monarchy, generations, and rebellion.

Across my wider research programme, the same concern with interpretation and power connects [digital history](/#digital-humanities), Indigenous cultural geography, environmental research, and [public engagement](/#projects). I combine close reading with GIS, data visualisation, and computer vision to recover Indigenous presence from historical maps. Collaborative work with museums, libraries, universities, and public bodies turns that research into exhibitions, digital resources, and policy contributions.

This movement between archival interpretation, spatial methods, and public practice is deliberate: each offers a different way to examine how knowledge is made, whose claims become visible, and how historical evidence travels beyond the academy.

I hold a PhD in History from the [University of Cambridge](https://www.hist.cam.ac.uk), an MSc in International Relations from the [S. Rajaratnam School of International Studies](https://www.rsis.edu.sg), and a joint BA/PhB in History from NUS and the Australian National University. I am Associate Editor of [*Cambridge Elements in Indigenous Environmental Research*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/publications/elements/elements-in-indigenous-environmental-research), an Associate Fellow of the Higher Education Academy and the Royal Historical Society, and a member of the AHRC Peer Review College.
